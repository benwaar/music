# Natural Noise

“Natural Noise” starts with something fully organic and chaotic – a field of grass – and runs it through a digital process that is meant to filter out noise. Instead of using the tools to make the image clearer, I push them until the subject almost disappears and what remains is the artifact of the filtering itself.

Grass is already pure complexity: millions of blades, overlapping, moving, throwing tiny shadows. When a denoising or smoothing algorithm tries to “clean” that, it has to decide what is signal and what is noise. The image that comes out of this struggle – the clumps, islands and voids left behind – is what interests me.

We’re no longer looking at the grass itself, but at how the system *thinks* about the grass. The shapes and territories in the image are the places where the algorithm decided, “this matters” or “this can be discarded.” The piece becomes a kind of CT scan or topographic map of the original photograph.

Conceptually, Natural Noise sits between landscape, abstraction and data art. It relates to artists who explore compression, blur and error (JPEG artifacts, motion blur, glitches) and treat those “technical flaws” as the main subject rather than a mistake to fix. It also connects to generative and code-based art where natural processes (growth, erosion, diffusion) are simulated as patterns.

**Core idea:**

- Nature already *is* noise.  
- When we “clean it up” with software, we don’t reveal truth; we reveal the aesthetic and biases of our filters.

Natural Noise is about that translation – how a living, shifting subject is reinterpreted when a digital system tries to simplify it.

# Subtle Systems – Practice Summary

“I use code to look at nature the way a machine does – as signal and noise. Grass fields, skies and small everyday landscapes are pushed through filtering processes until the subject almost disappears. What’s left is a map of what the system thinks is important.”

My work begins with quiet photographs: fields of grass, evening skies, telephone wires cutting across a sunset. I’m drawn to scenes where almost nothing happens, but colour and line still hold a charge.

From there I run the images through custom processes that treat them as data. Algorithms try to denoise, simplify or map what they see. The results become Natural Noise fields, grids and reliefs – abstractions that show the logic of the system as much as the original scene.

By pairing the subtle photograph with its processed counterpart, I’m not just decorating images with effects. I’m asking what a machine notices, what it discards, and how a landscape or a sky looks when it is turned into signal and noise. The work lives in the tension between those two views: the quiet, emotional world we stand inside, and the structured, almost clinical world our devices see.

# Triptych Plan (2 Main Structures)

## 1. Triptych Type A – “What the Field Becomes”

Each work = 3 images from the *same* source photo.

1. **Photograph**  
   - The original subtle image (grass field, water, trees, etc.), only lightly edited.

2. **Natural Noise**  
   - The processed, terrain-like version where the link to the photo is still visible: clumps, islands, bands, erosion.

3. **System / Grid**  
   - The full abstraction: grid, path or extruded relief derived from that same image.

**Display order:**  
Human view → machine filtering → pure system (left to right or top to bottom).

**Subjects to try first:**

- Grass / fields  
- Water surfaces  
- Trees / foliage  

Aim for 3–5 strong triptychs to form a clean series.

---

## 2. Triptych Type B – “Signal & Sky”

Built from photographs like the telephone-line sky.

1. **Signal & Sky – Photograph**  
   - Subtle sky + wires image. Minimal edits; keep the emotional, song-like mood.

2. **Signal & Sky – Natural Noise**  
   - Processed version where:
     - The sky becomes a noise field / gradient abstraction.  
     - The wires start to register as stronger bands, traces or disturbances.

3. **Signal & Sky – Map**  
   - A grid/line/path abstraction extracted from the same image or its noise version:
     - Emphasise vectors, routes, intersections – like a network diagram or map of calls.

**Effect:**  
One subject (sky + wires), three levels of reading:  
emotional view → machine perception → pure communication structure.

Aim for 2–4 of these triptychs as a parallel mini-series.

# Future Ideas

1. 10 Grass Studies
   - Take one grass photo and produce 10 distinct Natural Noise variations in one session. 
   - No judging, just output. Later, pick 2–3 that feel alive.

2. One Day, Three Subjects
   - Go out and shoot only: grass, water, and leaves.
   - That same day, run each through your process and create one final piece per subject.

3. Threshold Hunt
   - For a single photo, slide your parameters slowly and export a frame every time the image takes on a new, interesting structure.
   - Aim for a series of 6 images that show “the birth of abstraction.”

4. Black-and-White Week
   - For a week, only create grayscale Natural Noise images.
   - The lack of color forces you to focus on composition, density, and shape. After that, reintroduce color more intentionally.

5. Natural Noise Atlas
   - Choose 4–9 different natural textures (grass, leaves, water, rocks, sand, moss, etc.).
   - Apply the same Natural Noise process to each and present them together as a “map” or “atlas” of how different materials collapse under the same filter.

# Lenses & Questions

## Three Lenses

- **Machine Vision of Nature**  
  Am I showing how the *system* sees the scene?

- **Compression of Complexity**  
  What survives when the natural complexity is crushed?

- **Signal vs Noise as Landscape**  
  Where is the border between what’s kept and discarded, and can that border *be* the composition?

## After Making a Piece, Ask

1. What does this reveal that the original scene didn’t?  
2. Do I feel like I’m looking at the field, the filter, or a third thing?  
3. Would it be stronger with less colour / more contrast / different crop?  
4. If this hung in a gallery with no text, what three sentences would I want next to it?

Treat answering these as part of the artwork, not just admin.
