# Timeline of Generative Music Techniques

### 1. Stochastic Chance Games (1780s)
* **Technique:** *Musikalisches Würfelspiel* (Musical Dice Game).
* **How It Works:** Pre-composed musical measures are selected randomly using dice rolls to generate unique, structurally valid waltzes.
* **Key Figure:** Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart (attributed).
* **Significance:** The earliest popular framework treating music as a combinatorial system governed by probability.

### 2. Aleatoric & Indeterminate Systems (1950s)
* **Technique:** I Ching & Chance Operations.
* **How It Works:** Composers create strict procedural systems where pitch, duration, and dynamics are determined by throwing coins, using mathematical charts, or consulting the ancient Chinese *I Ching* text.
* **Key Figure:** John Cage (*Music of Changes*, 1951).
* **Significance:** Shifted the composer’s role from "author of sound" to "architect of a rule-based process".

### 3. Tape Loop Phasing (1965)
* **Technique:** Mechanical Phase-Shifting.
* **How It Works:** Identical magnetic tape loops are played simultaneously on different machines set to slightly different speeds. Over time, the loops drift out of phase, producing endlessly shifting micro-rhythms and evolving polyrhythms.
* **Key Figure:** Steve Reich (*It's Gonna Rain*, 1965; *Come Out*, 1966).
* **Significance:** Directly inspired Brian Eno's early work with physical tape mechanics.

### 4. Asynchronous Loop Systems (1975–1978)
* **Technique:** Multi-Tape Asynchrony.
* **How It Works:** Short acoustic phrases are recorded onto multiple tape loops of prime or unaligned lengths (e.g., 5 seconds, 7 seconds, 11 seconds). Running simultaneously, the loops overlap in combinations that take hours or days to repeat.
* **Key Figure:** Brian Eno (*Discreet Music*, 1975; *Ambient 1: Music for Airports*, 1978).
* **Significance:** Made ambient generative music famous, demonstrating how minimal inputs can yield rich, non-repeating sonic textures.

### 5. Algorithmic Rule-Based Systems (1994–1996)
* **Technique:** Software-Driven Generative Audio Engines.
* **How It Works:** MIDI engines use mathematical rules, probability weightings, scale constraints, and conditional triggers to generate note sequences dynamically without pre-recorded audio.
* **Key Figures:** Tim Cole & Pete Cole (SSEYO Koan software), Brian Eno (*Generative Music 1*, 1996).
* **Significance:** Eno coined the explicit term "Generative Music" with this release, moving the technique from physical hardware to real-time computer software.

### 6. Cellular Automata & Evolutionary Algorithms (2000s)
* **Technique:** Biological & Mathematical Simulation.
* **How It Works:** Software maps grid-based mathematical simulations (like Conway’s *Game of Life*) or genetic algorithms onto musical parameters (pitch, velocity, tempo), allowing dynamic patterns to "evolve" and mutate like living organisms.
* **Key Tools:** Max/MSP, Pure Data, SuperCollider.
* **Significance:** Enabled interactive generative systems that could react to live audio inputs or environmental sensors in real time.

### 7. Generative AI & Latent Space Synthesis (2016–Present)
* **Technique:** Neural Audio Synthesis & Deep Generative Models.
* **How It Works:** Large neural networks (Diffusion, Transformers, GANs) train on massive datasets to generate complex raw audio or symbolic music directly from text, latent space manipulation, or real-time conditioning.
* **Key Players:** Google (MusicLM), Meta (MusicGen), OpenAI (Jukebox), Stability AI.
* **Significance:** Evolved generative music from *rule-based parameter manipulation* (Eno style) to *statistical acoustic generation*, capable of synthesizing realistic instruments and vocals on the fly.
